The script

0:00

The problem

When a price feed goes quiet, every protocol depending on it takes a loss. Monitoring tells you it happened. Nobody carries the cost.

Attestable is parametric cover for infrastructure failure. A buyer and an underwriter agree terms up front, and the payout is decided by cryptographic proof of what happened on another chain — not by a claims process.

  1. Start on the site's top section. Don't scroll.
  2. Keep the cursor still until “buyer and an underwriter”, then hover the escrow figure.
0:25

A real outage, already paid

I didn't invent an outage. On the 31st of August, Chainlink's ETH/USD feed on Sepolia went silent for 773 minutes — nearly thirteen hours.

The policy allowed ninety. Here's the settlement: two hundred CTC to the buyer, twelve CTC of premium to the underwriter — because on a claim the underwriter still keeps what they were paid for taking the risk. And above them, a call into Attestcoin checking the outage was even provable before it agreed to pay. No human approved any of it.

  1. Switch to the Blockscout tab.
  2. Walk down the three rows: ChainInfo call, 200 CTC → buyer, 12 CTC → underwriter.
Stay on Internal txns. The Details tab shows Value: 0 CTC — settlement moves money through internal calls, so Details makes it look like nothing happened.
1:15

What one proof is

Every payout traces back to evidence, and each piece goes through four steps.

Chainlink published a price on Ethereum — we didn't put it there and can't influence it. Attestcoin proved it was in that block. Creditcoin's Block Prover verified the proof on-chain. Then our contract checked what a proof alone doesn't tell you: that the transaction succeeded, and that the emitter was the exact aggregator the policy names.

  1. Click Evidence Explorer.
  2. Move down steps 1 → 2 → 3 → 4, about a second each.
  3. Hover the block-number link on step 1 so the underline shows. Don't click it.
Don't select cover #1. Its gap figure is a scanner bug we found and fixed today — not a real outage.

Settle it live

This cover's window has closed. Right now the contract has no evidence for it, so it assumes the worst — it treats the whole eighty-minute window as one unbroken silence. Watch that number.

after the proof landsOne real Chainlink update, proven. The worst-case silence just fell from eighty minutes to forty-one, because we can now show the feed was alive in the middle of the window.

after it settlesHealthy. Every gap stayed inside tolerance, so the underwriter keeps the collateral and earns the premium. That outcome came from the contract — this interface computes nothing.

  1. Coverage → cover #6.
  2. Point at “80.0 min observed / 90.0 min allowed”.
  3. Fetch and submit evidence → approve in MetaMask. One prompt, ~30 seconds.
  4. Scroll up — it now reads 41.3 min. Rest the cursor there.
  5. Settle this cover → approve. Hold still when the badge flips to HEALTHY.
If anything misbehaves, switch to cover #4 — also verified settleable, also settles HEALTHY. Say: “a twenty-minute window can't contain a ninety-minute silence, so the contract proves health from the window alone.”
3:20

The claim path

This one I rigged deliberately, and I want to say so. A two-minute tolerance on a feed that publishes hourly is a guaranteed loss — no underwriter would ever write it. It's here to show the claim path executing on demand.

The economically real one is the thirteen-hour outage I showed you at the start.

  1. Click cover #5. Hover the CLAIMED badge.
  2. Point at the tolerance figure as you say “two-minute”.
Say this before a judge works it out. If they spot it first, everything else you claimed gets discounted.
4:00

What we don't claim

Most projects tell you what they prove. This panel also tells you what they don't.

We never claim Chainlink agreed to anything — they sign nothing and don't need to know we exist. We don't judge whether a price was correct; we cover silence, never inaccuracy. And we never claim to have proven a negative.

Anyone can deploy a contract that emits a fake price and get a perfectly genuine proof of it. The proof would be real and the evidence worthless. Checking the emitter is what separates the two — and we ran that exact attack against the live precompile. It was rejected.

  1. Click Trust Boundary.
  2. Sweep down the ✗ NOT CLAIMED column — the limits are the point.
  3. Stop moving. Hold two seconds. Cut.

If they ask

Why is the data on Ethereum at all?
Because it already is. Chainlink's node operators publish it there for their own reasons — we didn't create it, can't influence it, can't fake it. That's exactly what makes it credible evidence.
Is there really a market for this?
Honestly — unproven. The risk is measured and the loss mechanism is concrete, but nobody is hedging oracle-staleness today and we have no signed customers. Our next milestone isn't technical, it's one design partner: a lending protocol that's already been burned by oracle latency and remembers it.
What's left to build?
Multi-chain evidence in one policy. Mainnet is already attested and we confirmed the aggregator resolves — we couldn't get the archive access to finish it, so we cut it rather than ship something half-working.
What's the odd gap on cover #1?
A bug in our own evidence scanner, found today. It scanned a fixed block range instead of the policy's actual window, so it missed updates at the start and manufactured an outage that never happened. Both the worker and the browser now derive the range from the policy itself. We left that cover unsettled rather than settle on a figure we knew was wrong.
