02Product vision

Detection is a solved problem. Consequence isn't.

Every serious protocol already checks whether its price feed is stale. That check works. When the feed goes quiet, a lending protocol correctly halts liquidations.

And that is precisely when it starts losing money. Positions drift underwater, bad debt accrues, and the protocol absorbs it. Detection converted one loss into another. It prevented nothing.

Monitoring tells you that something went wrong.
Attestable lets someone carry the financial consequence of it.

Today this risk sits uninsured and unpriced on the balance sheet of every protocol that depends on an oracle. Risk committees cannot put a number on it, so they over-collateralise instead — which is expensive, and still doesn't pay out when it happens.

03What it is

A policy with exactly two parties — and the provider isn't one of them.

The buyer

A protocol exposed to a feed it doesn't control. Pays a premium up front. Converts an unbounded tail risk into a budgetable monthly cost.

The underwriter

Posts collateral, keeps the premium if the feed behaves. Earns yield that is uncorrelated with market direction.

The subject

Chainlink. Signs nothing, stakes nothing, receives nothing, and need not know we exist — exactly like weather in a rainfall derivative.

Why that matters commercially: there is no business-development bottleneck. We never need a provider's permission, signature, or integration to launch a market on their infrastructure. That is the difference between a product and a partnership deal.

05Technical alignment

Why this can only settle on Creditcoin.

The contract needs to answer two different questions, and Attestcoin is the only place both are answerable natively — with no bridge, no relayer quorum, and no trusted messenger.

Block Prover · 0x0FD2

“Did this happen?” Merkle inclusion against the source block's transaction trie, plus continuity down to a block the attestor set signed.

ChainInfo · 0x0FD3

“Could evidence have been supplied by now?” Settlement is blocked until the attestation frontier passes the window's end.

That second question is the one that makes an absence-of-evidence outcome honest. Without it, a slow attestor set — our own infrastructure lagging — would look identical to a real outage and could trigger a payout. We never claim to prove a negative. The finding is narrower and defensible: insufficient valid evidence arrived before an attestation-safe deadline.

Cryptography proves something happened; it cannot prove it was meaningful. Anyone can deploy a contract that emits a fabricated price and obtain a perfectly genuine proof of it. We check the emitter, the receipt status, and the event shape — and we ran that exact attack against the live precompile. It was rejected.

06Market fit

Who buys coverage.

They share one shape: their economics break when a feed they don't own goes quiet, and no amount of engineering on their side prevents it.

  • Lending protocols — the sharpest case. A correct staleness check makes them halt liquidations, so positions drift underwater and bad debt accrues.
  • Perps and derivatives venues — a stale mark price and funding rate means continuous bleeding to arbitrage for as long as the silence lasts.
  • Collateralised stablecoin issuers — stale collateral valuation threatens the peg, which is the product.
  • Vaults, structured products, RWA platforms — rebalancing and mark-to-market stop dead when the feed stops.
The buyer's real motive is not the payout. It is turning an unbounded tail risk into a line item. For the first time a risk committee can attach a number to “we depend on an oracle”.
07Market fit

Who underwrites it.

  • DeFi funds and yield desks — almost all DeFi yield is market beta. Oracle-liveness premium is uncorrelated with market direction, which is scarce. And because every update is public and timestamped, the risk is unusually modelable.
  • Market makers and volatility desks — already price tail events; this is cleaner data than most of what they trade.
  • The infrastructure operator itself — the strongest angle we found.

A newer oracle network competing with Chainlink can underwrite coverage on its own feed. “We pay you if we go stale” is a financial commitment, not a marketing claim.

That is also the cleanest answer to “why not just let the provider fix it?” — underwriting is how a provider proves they fixed it. It hands operators a procurement weapon they currently do not have.

08User base expansion

What each policy brings to Creditcoin.

Not a one-off transaction. A cover is an ongoing on-chain relationship for its entire life.

Two funded participants

A buyer and an underwriter, both needing CTC, who otherwise had no reason to transact on Creditcoin at all.

Recurring proof traffic

Every qualifying feed update is a separate verification transaction — continuous activity for the whole window, not a single settlement.

Escrow as TVL

Collateral and premium sit locked on-chain for the duration of every active cover.

And the reason they come is structural, not promotional: Creditcoin is the only place the contract can settle, because it is the only chain that natively verifies what happened on Ethereum. The demand isn't for a token — it's for a capability that exists nowhere else.

12Honesty

What we do not claim.

No signed customers

The risk is measured and the loss mechanism is concrete, but nobody is hedging oracle-staleness today. Every party in this deck is prospective.

We don't judge correctness

We cover silence, never inaccuracy. Judging whether a price was right would require an oracle — the dependency we exist to make survivable.

Trust isn't eliminated

It is relocated. Trust remains in Attestcoin's attestor set and in the feed's honesty about itself. This is a working prototype, not a regulated insurance product.

We state this plainly because the alternative is worse. A judge who catches you overclaiming discounts everything else you said — and the limits here are narrow enough that naming them costs us nothing.

13Prepared answers

The three questions we expect.

“Why is the data on Ethereum at all?”

Because it already is. Chainlink's node operators publish it there for their own reasons — we didn't create it, can't influence it, can't fake it. That's exactly what makes it credible evidence.

“Is there really a market for this?”

Honestly — unproven. The risk is measured and the loss mechanism is concrete, but nobody is hedging oracle-staleness today and we have no signed customers. Our next milestone isn't technical, it's one design partner: a lending protocol that's already been burned by oracle latency and remembers it.

“What's left to build?”

Multi-chain evidence in one policy. Mainnet is already attested and we confirmed the aggregator resolves — we couldn't get the archive access to finish it, so we cut it rather than ship something half-working.
